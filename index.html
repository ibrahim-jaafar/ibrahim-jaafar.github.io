About me

I design and build AI solutions, from automations and agents to complete internal platforms, that help teams work faster and more accurately.

I came to software through engineering. Years of designing and manufacturing products taught me to see work as a system: how it flows, where it breaks, and how to fix it at the root. That's how I approach every tool I build.

I've worked with AI since the first tools appeared, researching and testing what they could do and where they fell short, and that curiosity became my profession. I work methodically, with close attention to detail and clear documentation.

How I work

Discover

I sit with each person and have them walk me through their workflow: where their time goes, which tasks repeat, and which are hardest.

Analyse and research

I map every step in detail, find where the time and errors actually come from, and research the best available solutions for each problem before deciding what to build.

Plan

I plan every step, set the KPIs and limits, compare cost options, and wait for approval before building.

Build

I design the solution around how people already work, then build it and ship it to production.

Adopt

I train the team on each tool, document it, and keep improving it from their feedback and support tickets.

Blue House Films is a film and social media company of about 100 people. I joined in September 2026 as its AI Specialist, to help the team work faster and more accurately with less repetitive work. I started by replacing scattered tools and manual workflows with one connected internal platform, and shipped 9 tools to production in my first six weeks.

As the main developer on the 3-person R&D team, I own the platform end to end. I design and build the tools, host and secure the server, and set up the company's GitHub and automated backups. Ecosystem: one platform for the whole company

BeforeEvery employee had their own tools and their own API keys, with nothing connected. Onboarding a new person was a mess.
AfterOne connected platform and one source of truth. Every tool plugs into it as a plugin, and everything can be tracked.
  • Role-based access control: each employee sees only the data their role allows.
  • Plugin design, so each tool (HR, tasks, chat, mail) can be built and shipped on its own.
  • Self-hosted on an Oracle server with backups to Cloudflare R2.
  • Developer documentation I wrote: architecture guide, plugin guide, runbook and architecture decision records, so other developers can build plugins on their own. User guides are next.
  • On-laptop AI: plugins can run AI models on employees' own laptops. Models download into a shared cache and are used only if they match their fingerprint, and the engines that run them are built into the signed desktop app, so a web page can never introduce code.
  • Desktop app: signed releases with automatic updates, starts with the computer, keeps running in the tray, and shows unread counts on the taskbar or Dock. Windows and macOS.
  • Platform services: an audit log of every permission decision, a durable event system, scheduled jobs and reminders, alerts in the app or by email and Telegram, and recorded acceptance of each version of the terms of use.
  • Scale: about 70,000 lines of code, 1,200+ automated tests and 57 architecture decision records. I made 92% of the commits.

Mail

BeforeStaff had to log in to Bluehost webmail or set up email on their phones and Outlook, which was hard for non-technical people. Important emails were missed or answered late, and the company had faced phishing attacks with no protection beyond the basics.
AfterCompany email inside the platform with just an email and password, and instant notifications. Every message is checked for phishing and malicious content, and suspicious mail is clearly flagged.
  • Every sender labelled Internal, External or "Company, not verified", so spoofed company addresses stand out.
  • Warns when replies would go somewhere other than the sender, with one-click "Report phishing".
  • Checks sender authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), catches lookalike company domains (such as a "1" for an "l" or Cyrillic letters), flags risky attachments like macro-enabled files, and shows a dangerous link's real address before it opens. All checks run on our own server, with no outside service.
  • Remote images blocked by default, so senders can't track when an email is opened.

MoneyPrinterTurbo Extended: AI short-video generator

Python · FastAPI · React · CLIP / SigLIP-2 · Whisper · FFmpeg

Freelance project for a client who needed more control than the open-source MoneyPrinterTurbo gives. That tool writes a script, narrates it and assembles stock footage into a video. I replaced its interface with a React app and added AI matching between clips and script, so the footage fits what is being said. About 5,300 new lines of code, and the client was satisfied with the result.

  • Footage that matches meaning: the script is split into sentences, each candidate clip's frames are scored against the sentence with CLIP/SigLIP, and clips are assigned across the whole video at once (Hungarian algorithm) so the best matches aren't used up early.
  • AI script helpers: writes an attention-grabbing opening hook and turns each part of the script into precise footage search terms.
  • More footage sources: added YouTube (with browser-session sign-in), Wikimedia and Coverr alongside Pexels and Pixabay; every clip is trimmed and tagged with its source.
  • Clip-review editor: before rendering, the operator can swap a clip, search again with their own keywords, upload their own footage, stretch a clip across several sentences, trim it, see which clips are already used, then approve.
  • Speed: frame analyses are cached and computed in parallel in the background, and low-quality frames are scored down.
  • Voice and subtitles: Chatterbox voice cloning on the GPU and ElevenLabs, with word-level subtitle timing from faster-whisper.
  • A gallery of past clips, and automated tests for the new parts.

Polymarket trader analyser and strategy simulator

Python · WebSockets · Streamlit · Plotly · Polymarket and Binance APIs

Freelance project for a client who wanted to learn how successful traders work and test strategies safely. Enter a Polymarket account's wallet address and the tool pulls that trader's whole trade history, then breaks down how they trade. Strategies learned this way are then tested in simulators on live market data. It was used for research and simulation only, not live trading.

  • Trade collector: pulls an account's history from Polymarket's API in 15-minute windows, page by page, skipping duplicates, until it has the number of trades asked for.
  • Strategy analyser: per market or across all of them, it tells maker trades from taker trades, and charts entry prices, VWAP over time, and cumulative shares, profit and exposure, so the trader's strategy can be read from the data (Streamlit and Plotly).
  • Strategy simulator: a 10-state decision engine that trades on live prices and order books in dry-run mode, with built-in risk controls: limits on open positions, total exposure and new entries per update, no new entries near expiry, trend confirmation before entering, liquidity checks before buying, and profit targets that adjust as expiry approaches, with hedging to lock in profit when a trade turns. The full logic is documented in a written specification.
  • Market recorder and replay: records Bitcoin 15-minute up/down markets, with an interactive player to replay them, a live view of every open position, and a dashboard that replays each simulated market's trades and profit.

CurveMill: CNC file optimiser

Python · PySide6 · C# SolidWorks add-in

Internal tool for Ghandour Solutions, built to fix a bottleneck in the company's CNC workflow. It cleans and repairs DXF cutting files and previews how the cutting tool will run. On real files it cut sketch elements from 3,383 to 540, and a 7,014-line pattern down to 274.

  • Finds sketch faults (open profiles, duplicate lines, shared corner points) and shows each one as a clickable warning that zooms to it.
  • Keeps curves as true splines through import, editing and export, instead of breaking them into small lines.
  • Simulates the endmill cutting along the centre, inside or outside of a path, and repairs small gaps within a set tolerance.
  • A SolidWorks add-in with its own toolbar, plus a pass that rejoins broken curves into whole ones.
  • SolidWorks-style dimensions to place a pattern exactly on the board, a reusable pattern library, and project files to pause and resume a job.
  • About 16,000 lines of Python, covered by 225 automated tests.

Agency report automation (live demo)

Live · n8n · GoHighLevel API · Meta Conversions API · Gemini

A marketing report for two made-up agency clients, a skin clinic and a home-goods store. It pulls each client's leads and ad spend, sends conversions to Meta, checks the data for mistakes in plain code, and writes a short summary a client can read, for any day, week, month or year. Visitors can run it on clean data or break the data on purpose and see what the checks catch, explore the results on a client dashboard, and send test leads to watch them arrive.

  • Client dashboard: each client gets a dashboard like a real agency report: spend, cost per click, leads, cost per lead and results, each with its change (green when it's good news), a funnel from impressions to bookings or orders, spend and cost per lead for each campaign, spend against a budget, charts, data health, the leads behind each view and a CSV download. Day, week, month and year views open on the current period so far, updating live, with arrows to step back to earlier ones; they are added up from daily totals, with costs and rates worked out again from the sums, never averaged. Impressions, clicks and spend, the budgets and the history outside the run's 7 days are simulated and labelled as such.
  • Checks in code, not AI: leads with no source, duplicates written differently (+961 vs 0, capital letters), Meta and CRM not matching, totals that don't add up, unusually large orders and events Meta rejects. They run on whatever period is on screen, with the same code as the run.
  • Edge cases handled: an empty week shows "no leads" instead of dividing by zero, organic leads don't skew cost per lead, cancelled bookings are left out, and Arabic or emoji names survive hashing.
  • Private by design: the AI only sees totals and warnings, never names, phones or emails. Personal fields sent to Meta are SHA-256 hashed.
  • AI on request, kept honest: a summary of whatever period is on screen, written when asked; the server works out the figures itself. It never types a number; it writes placeholders, each next to its own label, and the code fills in the real values. Its answer must also follow a strict JSON schema. A broken answer is refused and a template summary is used instead.
  • Live: n8n workflows on a free server: one plays the customers of the last 7 days and saves them in a GoHighLevel sandbox; the report a real client would get reads them back, sends the conversions to Meta's Conversions API and posts an alert to Slack when a check fires or a run fails; another asks Gemini for a summary on request, and another sends test leads through GoHighLevel and Meta one at a time. The logic is covered by 198 automated tests.
